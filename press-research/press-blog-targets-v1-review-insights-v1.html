Press & blog targets v1: Insights review (insights-v1)

Reviewer: Insights Lead, for Dean Pryzmenski via Agency CEO and Comms & Creators. Reviewed 2026-11-14 ET. Input: press-blog-targets-v1 (CSV, MD, XLSX in /workspace/press-research/). Research only: no site was contacted, no form submitted, no sign-up, and no paid tool or API credential used. Public Semrush and Similarweb overview pages only.

Evidence labels: Verified-source = checked against the draft files. Verified-web = read on a public page this session (third-party traffic figures are still estimates). Inference = my reasoning. Unknown = not found.

Verdict

Usable with fixes. Not ready for Dean's sign-off as ranked. The math, links and Semrush figures check out. Three things need fixing first:

  1. Gaming outlets. Five gaming-first outlets sit in the ranking (PC Gamer is #3), and campaign locks exclude gaming framing.
  2. Fence wording. The 'Stream Deck alternative' label conflicts with the works-alongside fence.
  3. Mixed traffic sources. The ranking mixes two traffic sources that disagree by a median 51%, and its step bands amplify the gap.

Recommended method: Similarweb public as the single ranking source, a log-scaled traffic score, and gaming-first outlets on HOLD. Under that method, 12 of the top 15 stay. PC Gamer, GamesRadar+ and Notebookcheck drop out, and AppleInsider, Hackster.io and MacRumors come in.

Findings - Semrush is higher on 17 of 20 sites. Gaps run from −14% (9to5Mac) to +144% (Notebookcheck). Median +51% across all 20 and +64% across the top 15 (Verified-web figures; the summary statistics are my arithmetic). - The disagreement is not uniform. It is small for Hackaday (+9%), Macworld (+26%), The Verge (+30%) and Tom's Guide (+30%), and large for How-To Geek (+120%) and Notebookcheck (+144%). A flat correction factor would therefore be wrong (Inference). - Under the draft's step bands, four sites lose a full band on Similarweb: How-To Geek, XDA, Notebookcheck and Windows Central (Verified-source math).

Recommended method (Inference): 1. One source for ranking: Similarweb public. It is the only public source that covers every listed domain, because Semrush public pages return 404 for 13 of them. Keep Semrush as a cross-check column only. 2. Log-scaled traffic score instead of step bands: score = 1 + 4 × (log10(visits) − log10(10K)) ÷ (log10(20M) − log10(10K)), clipped to 1–5. With this score, a 2× source disagreement moves a site's composite by about 0.15, against 0.4 for one band step under the draft. The ordering stops hinging on which tool was used. 3. Keep the 60/40 fit/traffic weights. Keep a missing traffic figure as 'Unknown' and excluded, never scored as the floor.

Q2. Wirecutter (shares nytimes.com)

  • Evidence (Verified-web): Semrush and Similarweb public overviews are domain-level only, so they show nytimes.com, not /wirecutter/. No public subfolder estimate was found (Unknown).
  • Evidence (Verified-web, publisher-stated via press): a Mediagazer summary dated May 12, 2026 of a Nieman Lab piece says Wirecutter 'more than tripled its traffic to 15M monthly readers' (https://mediagazer.com/260512/p4). That is a publisher-stated readers figure, not a third-party visits estimate, so it is not comparable with the other rows.
  • Recommendation (Inference): rank Wirecutter on fit only, with a flag. Exclude it from the traffic weight: composite = fit (3.0), shown as 'traffic N/A, publisher states ~15M monthly readers'. Do not feed 15M into the formula and do not score it at the floor. Sensitivity only: if the 15M were treated as visits, its composite would be about 3.7 (rank ~29 of 48). It also accepts no sponsored content and chooses products independently (draft, Verified-source), so it is a long-lead editorial target whatever its rank.

Q3. Accessibility sites (6K–121K visits)

  • Recommendation (Inference): move them to a separate 'Accessibility & assistive tech' list with its own ranking. Do not leave them at #31–46 of a reach-weighted list, where any top-N cut drops them.
  • Why: accessibility is a core Rebind mission pillar, not a reach play. Readers of AppleVis, AbilityNet and AFB AccessWorld are assistive-tech users and advisers who act on remapping and automation guidance and pass it on through communities and organisations. Web visits understate that reach (podcasts, newsletters, forums, charity networks), so a reach score measures the wrong thing for this group (Inference).
  • Suggested rubric for that list: fit (1–5) 50%, audience match (AT users or advisers) 30%, editorial path (reviews or accepts submissions) 20%. Show traffic as context only. Can I Play That? stays listed but marked 'closed to pitches' (draft, Verified-source).
  • Fences for this list: no medical or RSI claims. AppleVis is largely an iOS audience, so use only the approved mobile line, 'Android and iOS apps are in development', with no dates or features. The accessible-gaming sites (Can I Play That?, AbleGamers, Access-Ability) need a Dean decision because of the gaming lock.

Errors and fence issues in the draft

Must-fix 1. Gaming fence: Tier 2 is named 'Enthusiast / gaming hardware', and PC Gamer (#3), GamesRadar+ (#11), IGN (#22), Rock Paper Shotgun (#32) and Kotaku (#35) are gaming-first outlets. Campaign locks exclude gaming framing. Move these five to a HOLD list pending Dean, rename the tier 'PC hardware & peripherals', and remove the 'PC gaming & peripherals' niche tag from all rows. 2. Accessible-gaming sites (Can I Play That?, AbleGamers, Access-Ability) also carry gaming framing. Flag them for a Dean decision. If kept, use an accessibility and remapping angle only, with no medical claims. 3. 'Stream Deck alt.' niche label and 'Stream Deck alternative' wording imply Rebind replaces Stream Deck. Fence: Rebind works alongside Stream Deck, AutoHotkey, PowerToys, QMK, VIA and Keyboard Maestro. Rename the label to 'Stream Deck & control surfaces'. 4. Mixed traffic sources: 37 rows use Semrush and 11 use Similarweb. On the 20 sites I re-checked on both, Semrush reads a median 51% higher than Similarweb (64% across the top 15), so Similarweb-sourced rows (AppleInsider, PCWorld, Digital Trends, KitGuru, MacStories, accessibility sites) are scored low. Use one source. 5. Band cliffs: the step bands (≥10M = 5, ≥3M = 4, ≥1M = 3) turn small source gaps into a 1-point traffic swing (0.4 composite). How-To Geek, XDA, Notebookcheck and Windows Central each drop a band on Similarweb. Ranks 1–8 are all tied at 5.0 and ordered only by Semrush traffic. 6. Wirecutter: its composite uses the floor traffic score (1), which treats missing data as tiny reach. It should be flagged and excluded from traffic weight instead (see Q2). 7. AFB AccessWorld: the 86.4K figure is for the whole afb.org domain, not /aw. Keep the flag and treat it as an upper bound (Inference).
