1. How to use this briefing

Read this before you draft or revise a creator letter, and before you mark any letter draft-ready.

Then do the work in this order, using the named skills in section 12:

  1. Load agency-os and writing-team.
  2. Restate the job with marketing-campaign-brief.
  3. Gather evidence (including real, public channel knowledge) before stylish prose.
  4. Build a claim ledger.
  5. Draft in the form the brief assigns. For outreach, write as Dean: a human letter, not a landing page and not a press release. For longer explainers, use journalistic-marketing-writing.
  6. Fact-check every number, product claim, and creator-specific detail against sources you opened.
  7. Run marketing-claim-fence-qa.
  8. Package the file for review. Status stays READY FOR DEAN / NOT LIVE, or, for this training file, READY FOR CONTENT & COPY / NOT LIVE.

If two letters would still read as the same letter after you changed the creator's name and one compliment, stop and rewrite. That is the failure mode this briefing exists to kill.

3. Why this briefing exists

Dean is reviewing YouTuber outreach drafts until he is happy. He asked for research into the creators and for Content & Copy to be taught to write much better.

The pattern to unlearn [Verified-source]: letters assembled from shared stock paragraphs, with only a custom middle sentence swapped. That method produces mail that a creator can smell as mail-merge. It also produces the prose vices this briefing cites from public sources: ready-made phrases that think for you, vague nouns where a specific detail should be, and a tone that pretends intimacy it has not earned.

A good letter has required ingredients. A templated letter treats those ingredients as a skeleton and sprinkles a compliment on top. Your job is the opposite: start from one creator's public work, write a letter that could not be sent to anyone else, and then seat the required legal and product facts inside that letter without flattening the voice.

[Inference] This is a craft standard, not a promise that better letters will produce a particular reply rate. See section 14.

4. What makes writing clearly good

Good writing is not ornament. It is the shortest honest path from a specific fact to a specific reader. The sources below agree on the same cluster of habits, even though they were written for different jobs: essays, student prose, government pages, and product content.

4.1 Let the meaning choose the word

George Orwell, in "Politics and the English Language" (Horizon, April 1946), argues that stale, prefabricated language arrives faster than thought, and that the writer should decide what is meant before hunting for words. He writes that "What is above all needed is to let the meaning choose the word, and not the other way about." [Verified-web] Orwell Foundation text of the essay, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

He asks a scrupulous writer to test every sentence with questions that are still the right ones for this desk: What am I trying to say? What words will express it? What image or idiom will make it clearer? Is this image fresh enough to have an effect? Could I put it more shortly? Have I said anything that is avoidably ugly? [Verified-web] Same source.

When instinct fails, he offers six rules. Quote them as rules of last resort, not as a costume:

  1. Never use a metaphor, simile or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print.
  2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
  3. If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.
  4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
  5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
  6. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous.

[Verified-web] Same Orwell Foundation page. The Foundation notes that the essay remains under copyright in some jurisdictions, including the United States, and is reproduced there with permission of the Orwell Estate. Do not paste or reconstruct the essay. Use the rules; do not mimic Orwell's political examples in outreach copy.

Orwell's most useful warning for a writing desk under deadline is mechanical: modern bad prose, he says, consists in "gumming together long strips of words which have already been set in order by someone else." [Verified-web] Same source. That is exactly what a shared stock paragraph is.

4.2 Prefer the specific noun and the earned detail

William Strunk, Jr., in the public-domain The Elements of Style (Harcourt, Brace and Company edition on Project Gutenberg), states the principle directly: "Prefer the specific to the general, the definite to the vague, the concrete to the abstract." [Verified-web] Project Gutenberg, eBook 37134, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

His demonstration is the one to memorize. Not "A period of unfavorable weather set in," but "It rained every day for a week." Not "He showed satisfaction as he took possession of his well-earned reward," but "He grinned as he pocketed the coin." [Verified-web] Same source, Rule 12.

Strunk's reason is not decoration. He quotes Herbert Spencer's point that readers do not think in generals; they translate abstract words back into particular images, which costs time and force. A specific term hands them the image at once. [Verified-web] Same source.

For this desk, that rule has a local translation [Inference]:

Vague (cut it) Specific (write this kind of thing, and only if true)
"your awesome content" "the part of Title of Video where you remap Caps Lock to Escape and then show the one case that breaks"
"powerful automation" "a Luau script on the computer that, after you install the app and then add the script, sends the keystroke you bound"
"next-level productivity" "the same shortcut on Windows and on a Mac, within the limits in section 6"
"innovative hardware" "Rebind Link, a USB device, if they are in the US or Canada and the draft offer applies"
"I love your channel" a fact from one public video, named by title, that a stranger could check

Earned detail is detail you can point to. Unearned detail is atmosphere: "I've been watching for years," "your community is amazing," "this would be perfect for your audience." Those sentences can be dropped onto any letter. They do no work.

Strunk also warns writers off hollow praise-words. He tells them to avoid interesting as a perfunctory introduction ("Instead of announcing that what you are about to tell is interesting, make it so") and to use very sparingly, preferring words that are strong in themselves. He calls one of the most a threadbare opening. [Verified-web] Same Gutenberg text, Chapter V.

4.3 Cut empty words, empty superlatives, and fake urgency

Strunk's Rule 13: "Vigorous writing is concise. A sentence should contain no unnecessary words, a paragraph no unnecessary sentences, for the same reason that a drawing should have no unnecessary lines and a machine no unnecessary parts." This does not mean all sentences must be short, or that detail should be stripped out. It means every word should tell. [Verified-web] Same Gutenberg text.

He tells writers to revise the fact that out of sentences (owing to the fact that becomes because). He treats character and nature as padding (acts of a hostile character becomes hostile acts). [Verified-web] Same source.

GOV.UK's content guidance makes the same demand in plainer clothes. It tells writers not to use a long formal word when a short one will do: buy not purchase, help not assist, about not approximately. It warns that words ending in -ion and -ment often lengthen a sentence without adding meaning. It tells writers to break a buzzword into the action they are actually taking, and to "be open and specific." [Verified-web] Use clear language, GOV.UK content and publishing guidance, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

The GOV.UK A to Z list of words to avoid is a useful kill-list for marketing fog. Do not write utilise, leverage, robust, transform, empower, facilitate, incentivise, overarching, going forward, or in order to when a shorter, more specific verb exists. Do not write that Rebind will deliver an abstract improvement; pizzas are delivered, not "next-level workflows." [Verified-web] GOV.UK A to Z style guide, "Words to avoid", retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

Fake urgency is a cousin of empty superlatives. Do not invent deadlines, remaining units, "this week only," or implied scarcity for a draft outreach letter. There is no authorized send, and there is no authorized shipment. [Verified-source] A sentence that hurries the reader toward a gift they have not been offered in life is dishonest, which is also a prose problem: Orwell calls insincerity "the great enemy of clear language." [Verified-web] Orwell Foundation page cited above.

4.4 Use the active voice, then vary the rhythm

Active voice is not a personality tic. It is the default of every serious plain-language source opened for this briefing.

  • Strunk: "The active voice is usually more direct and vigorous than the passive." I shall always remember my first visit to Boston beats My first visit to Boston will always be remembered by me. He does not ban the passive; he says it is often convenient and sometimes necessary, especially when you need a particular noun as the subject. [Verified-web] Gutenberg text, Rule 10.
  • Digital.gov: "Use the active voice. Active voice helps the message stay clear and easy-to-read." [Verified-web] Principles of plain language, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.
  • GOV.UK: active voice "is more direct and puts the focus on the user and the action they need to take." Example: "You need an electronic travel authorisation" is easier than "An electronic travel authorisation is needed." The guidance also names cases where passive can work, including when the outcome matters more than the actor. [Verified-web] GOV.UK clear-language page cited above.
  • Mailchimp's public style guide lists active voice first among its style tips, and tells writers to use positive language rather than negative language. [Verified-web] Voice and Tone, Mailchimp Content Style Guide, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

Rhythm is the next skill after voice. Strunk's Rule 14 warns against a succession of loose sentences of the same shape: clause, and or but, clause. "Although single sentences of this type may be unexceptionable," he writes, "a series soon becomes monotonous and tedious." The fix is not random variation for its own sake. Recast enough sentences into simple sentences, semicolon sentences, or longer periodic sentences "whichever best represent the real relations of the thought." [Verified-web] Gutenberg text, Rule 14.

He also says to place the emphatic words of a sentence at the end, and that this principle applies to words in a sentence, sentences in a paragraph, and paragraphs in a composition. [Verified-web] Gutenberg text, Rule 18.

GOV.UK adds a practical length check: try to split sentences over 25 words, and keep paragraphs to no more than five sentences. It immediately qualifies that length is not the real test: "If you write only a single paragraph but it's full of jargon, then it's too long." [Verified-web] GOV.UK clear-language page.

[Inference] For a one-page outreach letter, that means: mix a short sentence with a longer one; do not write seventeen letters that are all five medium paragraphs of identical cadence; put the load-bearing fact (the named video, the true example, the actual ask) where the sentence lands, not in a subordinate haze.

4.5 Write for one reader, in language that reader already uses

Digital.gov states the first rule of plain language as "write for your audience," and rejects the myth that plain language means dumbing down. Use language the actual reader understands. Do not write for an eighth-grade class if the readers are specialists; do not write for specialists' jargon if the reader is not one. [Verified-web] Digital.gov principles page.

GOV.UK reports that specialist readers also prefer plain English because they can understand the information faster, and cites research on legal language in which 80 percent of people preferred sentences written in clear English, with the preference stronger as the issue became more complex and as the reader's education and specialist knowledge increased. [Verified-web] GOV.UK clear-language page. That page does not name the underlying study in the text retrieved on 8 November 2026, so treat the statistic as GOV.UK's reported finding, not as a study you independently opened.

For creator letters, the audience is one named YouTuber who already teaches tools, shortcuts, firmware, or desk setups. Write in the vocabulary of that work: the key, the layer, the software they already name, the problem they already demonstrated. Do not translate their craft into marketing dialect (unlock your potential, seamless ecosystem, game-changing productivity suite). [Inference]

Mailchimp's writing principles are a clean desk motto, even though they were written for Mailchimp's own product content: empower, respect, educate, guide, and speak truth. "Avoid dramatic storytelling and grandiose claims. Focus on our real strengths." Content should be clear, useful, friendly, and appropriate. "Don't market at people; communicate with them." "Tell readers what they need to know, not just what we want to say." [Verified-web] Writing Goals and Principles, Mailchimp Content Style Guide, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

Mailchimp also separates voice from tone: voice stays stable; tone changes with the reader's situation. Clarity comes before entertainment, and forced humor is worse than none. They describe themselves as plainspoken on purpose, because customers live in "one muddled by hyperbolic language, upsells, and over-promises." [Verified-web] Mailchimp Voice and Tone page. Rebind letters should take the same side: strip the hyperbole, keep the human.

4.6 Honesty is a prose technique, not only a legal one

Orwell's line that insincerity is the great enemy of clear language is the moral center of this section. [Verified-web] Orwell Foundation page. A writer who does not quite believe the sentence reaches for longer words and exhausted idioms.

On this desk, insincerity has local forms [Inference]:

  • Fake-fan voice: "I've been a huge fan for years" when the writer watched one video this afternoon, or watched none.
  • Fake intimacy: "your community means the world to us."
  • Fake consensus: "creators like you love Rebind" with no named, authorized proof.
  • Fake completeness: implying the app does everything a Stream Deck, AutoHotkey script, or QMK keymap already does.
  • Fake medical or accessibility outcomes.
  • Fake ease: implying that installing the app installs a script, or that any AutoHotkey script can be dropped in or auto-ported.

Put statements in positive form when you can (Strunk, Rule 11: tell the reader what is, not only what is not). But when a limit is the truth, state the limit next to the promise. That is honesty and it is also clearer. [Verified-web] Gutenberg Rule 11; [Verified-source] product fences in section 6.

4.7 A short anti-glossary for this desk

Cut or rewrite these on sight [Inference], informed by the sources above:

  • game-changing, revolutionary, next-level, best-in-class, seamless, robust, leverage, utilize, unlock, supercharge, elevate your workflow
  • I hope this email finds you well
  • I came across your profile
  • I've been following your content for a while (unless you then prove it with a checkable detail, and even then prefer the detail without the courtship)
  • your content aligns perfectly with our brand values
  • we would love to explore a partnership with no actual ask
  • limited time, don't miss out, only a few left in a draft letter that cannot ship anything
  • replaces AutoHotkey / Keyboard Maestro / PowerToys / QMK / VIA / Stream Deck

5. How the writing desk should work

Do not freelance a letter because the ingredients are known. Run the desk. This sequence is required by writing-team and agency-os. [Verified-source]

Step 1. Brief

Use marketing-campaign-brief. A brief is ready when it names one problem, one audience, one behavior, one main promise, the proof, and the exclusions. If two strategies would both satisfy the brief, it is still too vague.

For a creator letter, the brief should lock at least:

  • Who the letter is from: Dean, who works on Rebind, on the marketing team.
  • Who it is to: one named creator.
  • The job: a draft letter Dean can review, not a send.
  • The promise: an honest introduction to Rebind, plus one small true example that fits that creator.
  • The optional Link offer and its terms, if the brief includes it.
  • The single low-friction reply CTA.
  • Holds: files only; ads HOLD; no email, post, or shipment.
  • Fences: section 6 of this briefing, plus current Content Rules.

Restate the brief in your own words before you draft. If a product fact is unknown, block that claim. Do not fill the gap with inference presented as fact.

Step 2. Evidence

Source before style. For outreach, evidence has two piles:

  1. Product facts from the approved dossier, this briefing's fences, and pages you actually open (for example the install URL).
  2. Creator facts from public work you actually opened: a video title, a format they repeat, a tool they teach, a problem they demonstrated. Record the URL, the retrieved date, and the exact sentence or moment the source supports.

Do not invent that Dean uses their overlays, owns their merch, or has "been a subscriber since the beginning."

Step 3. Claim ledger

Before stylish prose, list every objective claim the letter will make. Give each an ID, the exact wording you intend, the supported statement, the evidence label, and the source. Include creator-specific claims ("in Video Title you show X") and product claims ("local scripts do not require hardware").

If a claim cannot earn a label and a source, cut it.

Step 4. Draft

Write the letter as Dean. Open with the required identity facts. Then let this creator's public work choose the example, the vocabulary, and the rhythm. Include every required ingredient from section 8. Do not start from a shared paragraph file and swap a line.

Step 5. Fact-check

Read the draft against the ledger. Every number needs a citation in the working file (it need not clutter the letter itself). Check titles of videos, names of tools, OS limits, hardware limits, the install URL, and the offer terms. If you mention price, either re-verify the shop page this turn or clearly label the figure as prior team retrieval dated 2026-11-08. [Verified-source]

Prior team retrieval dated 2026-11-08, not independently re-verified in the research for this training file:

  • Rebind Link: $140 US/CA
  • BYOH: $90 (needs a Teensy 4.0 or 4.1, about $25)
  • Rebind Pro: $9/month or $72/year

If a later approved dossier supersedes these figures, use the later figures and say so.

Step 6. Claim-fence QA

Run marketing-claim-fence-qa. Extract every promise, comparison, compatibility claim, number, quote, and implied result. Fail the draft for any item in that skill's fail list, including invented features or testimonials, "replaces" language for the named tools, universal hardware-free claims, medical claims, app-install treated as script-install or activation, TypeTation destinations, unsupported OS claims, pointer movement / F13–F24 / media keys promised without Link or BYOH, complete Mac window control, RebindGPT implied to be free, fake-user voice, or undisclosed promotion.

Do not soften a false claim into vague hype. Cut it or qualify it until it is true.

Step 7. Package for review

Save a versioned source file and, when the deliverable is a package, a self-contained HTML viewing copy. Status is READY FOR DEAN / NOT LIVE. Approving a draft does not authorize sending it. Do not message Dean or other lanes to resolve scope; report to Agency CEO.

End a proposal in Suggest mode: why it is proposed, the expected read, and a request to approve, deny, or revise.

6. Product-truth fences

These are standing facts for Rebind copy. Do not contradict them. [Verified-source] unless a newer approved dossier supersedes them.

  • Product: Rebind.gg is a device-macro, automation, accessibility, and everything-PC multitool. The app is free. Scripts are Luau and run on the computer.
  • OS: Windows 10/11 64-bit and macOS 13+ Apple Silicon only. No Linux. No Intel Mac claim.
  • Local scripts do not require hardware or an account, but not every capability works without hardware.
  • Installing the app does not install a marketplace script. That is a second step. Activation is the named script doing the visible thing, not app install and not a welcome tour.
  • Default install URL: https://www.rebind.gg/install
  • Use-both framing with AutoHotkey, Keyboard Maestro, PowerToys, QMK, VIA, and Stream Deck. Never say Rebind replaces them.
  • Rebind is not a medical device. Do not claim guaranteed accessibility outcomes.
  • Many features are USB-only.
  • F13–F24, media keys, and pointer movement need Link or BYOH hardware.
  • Mac window control is partial.
  • RebindGPT is a Pro feature.
  • TypeTation is excluded. Do not send campaign traffic or destinations to it.
  • Do not claim Rebind can copy any existing AutoHotkey scripts or scripts from similar Lua-based peripheral software. No .ahk drop-in. No automatic AI porting.
  • Compare alternatives with use-both framing, including device apps such as Logitech G HUB, Corsair iCUE, Razer Synapse, or SteelSeries GG when they are relevant to the creator's work.

If the letter offers hardware, remember: not every feature works without it, and the free app is still the start of the story. Do not let the gift overstate what the plastic does.

7. Creator outreach craft

7.1 What public guidance actually says

A full Harvard Business Review article on cold email, Tucker Max, "A Guide to Cold Emailing" (20 September 2016), was opened for this briefing but the retrieved page showed only the opening. The visible claim: cold email is harder than most communication because you have no relationship yet and you lack non-verbal feedback, so you cannot adjust in real time, and "most cold emails fail." [Verified-web] HBR, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York. The rest of that article was not available in the retrieval. Do not invent further Max quotations or a reply-rate statistic from memory.

A second HBR piece, Bryan A. Garner, "Write E-Mails That People Won't Ignore" (21 February 2013), was likewise paywalled after the lede. The visible claim: recipients do not have time to engage fully with every email; some receive hundreds a day; they start with the ones they can deal with quickly and may never get to the rest. [Verified-web] HBR, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

Shopify's public "How To Write an Influencer Outreach Email" (by Shopify Staff, 2 September 2026) is vendor guidance, not proof that the tactics work. Use it as a description of common advice, labeled [Verified-web] as a claim. It says an outreach email should include a clear subject line, a personalized opener, a brief explanation of the brand and why this creator, a direct collaboration ask, a clear next step, and contact information. It recommends a warm, human tone rather than a generic sales tone. It reports advice from store owners to stay concise (one quoted recommendation: long enough to cover the good parts, short enough to keep it interesting, with a suggested ceiling of 200 words), to make a simple ask, and to research the recipient for a real commonality. [Verified-web] Shopify, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York.

Treat Shopify's own sample templates as a warning as well as a resource. Several of them share the same skeleton and leave a bracket where a compliment goes: "I've been following you on [platform] for a while now, and am a huge fan of [specific compliment]." [Verified-web] Same page. That is the failure mode Dean is reviewing against, dressed up as best practice. A bracket is not personalization.

Shopify also states that "Research shows that most cold outreach emails receive reply rates of between only 1% and 8.5%." The retrieved page did not name or link the study. [Unknown] Do not repeat that range as a fact in client-facing copy or as a forecast for Rebind.

[Inference] What survives after you discard unverified metrics: the letter has to earn a stranger's time in the first lines; it should be specific to them; it should say who you are, what Rebind is, why this note exists, and what a reply would mean; it should be short enough to answer quickly, which matches Garner's visible point that people start with mail they can deal with quickly.

7.2 Use real channel knowledge so the letter cannot be sent to anyone else

Before you write, open public work and take notes a second writer could check.

Minimum research, per creator:

  1. Channel name, person name if they use one, and the public contact path the brief specifies. Do not hunt private addresses outside the brief.
  2. One specific video (title, URL, date if shown, retrieved date). Write two or three concrete sentences about what happens in it: a tool, a format, a problem, a joke, a constraint they admit.
  3. One repeated format, if they have one: teardown, vs-comparison, desk tour, firmware explainer, audience Q&A, livestream recap.
  4. One tool or workflow they already teach: AutoHotkey, Keyboard Maestro, PowerToys, QMK, VIA, Stream Deck, a vendor device app, macOS Shortcuts, and so on.
  5. One honest fit or non-fit with Rebind. If the honest fit is thin, say so in the working notes and write a smaller letter, or flag that this creator may not be the right recipient. Do not invent a tighter fit.
  6. What you will not claim: watch time, revenue, "your audience would love this," subscriber counts you did not verify, or that they already need hardware they have not discussed.

Then apply the swap test [Inference]:

Change the creator's name and delete the most specific sentence. Could you send the remainder to a different person on the list with only cosmetic edits? If yes, the letter is still a template.

Load-bearing specificity looks like this:

  • You name a video title a reader can search.
  • You describe a move they actually demonstrated (a layer, a key, a software pairing, a frustration).
  • Your Rebind example answers that same class of problem, with fences attached.
  • A different creator who makes different videos would force a different example, a different vocabulary, and often a different paragraph order.

Weak specificity looks like this:

  • "Loved your recent video on productivity."
  • "Your editing style is so unique."
  • "You clearly care about your audience."
  • "This would fit your content perfectly."

Those sentences fail the swap test.

Do not pretend to be a superfan. It is honest to say you looked at a named public video while writing. It is not honest to borrow the history of a long-time viewer. Mailchimp's principle applies: speak truth; do not market at people. [Verified-web] Mailchimp writing-principles page.

7.3 Keep required legal and disclosure ingredients without making the body feel templated

Some facts must appear in every letter that includes the optional Link offer. The meanings are not creative raw material. The joinery is.

Locked meanings [Verified-source]:

  • The file is a draft. It is not live. It is not to be sent until Dean authorizes a named send.
  • The optional gifted Rebind Link offer is draft copy only. It is not shipment authorization.
  • The offer, when used, is US and Canada only.
  • No video is required.
  • Terms come after they have used it.
  • The creator keeps editorial control.
  • If they publish after receiving a gift, they should disclose the gift.

Those meanings also match public US advertising guidance on material connections. The Electronic Code of Federal Regulations, 16 CFR Part 255, "Guides Concerning Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising" (source note: 88 FR 48102, 26 July 2023), says a connection that might materially affect the weight or credibility of an endorsement, and that the audience would not reasonably expect, must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Material connections "can include monetary payment or the provision of free or discounted products ... regardless of whether the advertiser requires an endorsement in return." On social media or the internet, the disclosure should be unavoidable. [Verified-web] eCFR Title 16, Part 255, retrieved 8 November 2026, America/New_York. Consumer-facing FTC pages titled "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers" and "FTC's Endorsement Guides: What People Are Asking" returned HTTP 404 when fetched on 8 November 2026, so this briefing relies on the eCFR text rather than those FAQs.

The same part says advertisers should provide guidance to endorsers about misleading statements and unexpected material connections, monitor compliance, and take action to remedy non-compliance. [Verified-web] 16 CFR 255.1(d), same eCFR page.

Example 7 in 16 CFR 255.5 is the closest official illustration to a hardware gift: a tool manufacturer sends a woodworking influencer an expensive lathe in the hope of a post; if a significant minority of viewers would not know the lathe was free, the influencer should clearly and conspicuously disclose receiving it for free; the manufacturer should advise the influencer of that when it provides the lathe and should have reasonable monitoring procedures. [Verified-web] Same eCFR page.

Endorsements must reflect the honest opinions of the endorser, and an endorsement may not convey a claim that would be deceptive if the advertiser made it directly. [Verified-web] 16 CFR 255.1(a). That is why Rebind letters must not feed creators claims we would not be allowed to make ourselves (medical results, "replaces AutoHotkey," Linux support, drop-in .ahk conversion, and the rest of section 6).

How to write the required facts without cloning seventeen bodies [Inference]:

  1. Lock meaning, vary joinery. Do not rewrite "US and Canada only" into something fuzzier, and do not "creatively" drop editorial control. You may change what sentence the term sits in, what example precedes it, and whether it appears as two short sentences or one careful paragraph.
  2. Do not create a legal appendix that is visually identical in every file. A gray block of identical compliance text teaches the reader that everything above it was also boilerplate. Seat the terms in ordinary prose, in Dean's voice.
  3. Put identity first, terms where the offer appears. When the letter reaches the optional Link, say what is being offered, then immediately say what is not being asked. That pairing (optional gift next to no video required and you keep editorial control) reads as respect, not as a footer.
  4. Use plain words for disclosure. "If you publish something after receiving one, please disclose that it was a gift" is clearer than "material connection" in the letter itself. Keep the legal vocabulary in the working notes and QA ledger.
  5. Do not over-explain FTC chapter and verse to the creator. They need the practical duty. The eCFR citations belong in your QA file.
  6. Never imply that a thank-you post, a review, or a mention is expected. "No video required" and "editorial control" have to survive the net impression of the whole letter. A wink ("no pressure, but your audience would go crazy") cancels the term.

7.4 Vary sentence rhythm and examples across 17 letters

Seventeen honest letters will resemble one another in identity and in offer terms. They must not resemble one another in the sentences that do the seeing.

Vary these things, on purpose [Inference]:

  • The hook. One letter can open from a video title. Another can open from a format they repeat. Another can open from a tool they teach and a limit they already named. Do not open all seventeen with "Hello, my name is Dean. I loved your recent video about…"
  • The true example. Match the example to the creator. A QMK layer video invites a different Rebind example than a Keyboard Maestro palette video or a Stream Deck profile video. If you reuse the same example in more than two letters, you are probably not looking hard enough.
  • Sentence length. Follow Strunk: avoid a run of the same loose shape. Follow GOV.UK: split sentences that crawl past 25 words, but do not turn every letter into identical staccato.
  • Where the weight sits. Strunk puts emphasis at the end. Some letters should land on the named video. Some should land on the limit you are being honest about. Some should land on the low-friction question.
  • Paragraph count and order. The required ingredients can appear in a slightly different sequence after the identity opening, as long as none are missing and the offer terms stay attached to the offer.
  • The CTA wording. The job of the CTA is constant (one low-friction reply). The words can change: a question about whether they want the draft offer, a question about whether the example even fits their setup, an invitation to say "no thanks." Do not invent extra asks (a call, a rate card, a content calendar) unless the brief includes them.
  • Subject lines, when the package includes them. Specific and calm. No fake urgency. No "Collaboration Opportunity." No "Quick question" unless the letter is, in fact, one short question.

Do not vary these things:

  • Who Dean is.
  • Product fences.
  • Offer geography, disclosure, editorial control, "no video required," draft status, or "not a shipment authorization."
  • The rule that you will not send the letter.

A useful revision trick: read two finished drafts back to back, covering the names. If you cannot tell them apart, one of them is still stock.

8. Required letter ingredients

Every YouTuber outreach draft in this program must include the following [Verified-source]:

  1. Open as Dean. Include these facts early: hello; my name is Dean; he works on Rebind, on the marketing team. The facts are required. The melody is not. You may write "Hello — my name is Dean. I work on the marketing team at Rebind." You may not hide who is writing, invent a different sender, or open with three paragraphs of flattery before the name.

  5. One low-friction reply CTA. One ask. Easy to answer in a sentence. No stacked questions. No "let's hop on a quick call" unless the brief says so.

Working-file marks, not for the creator-facing body unless the package format requires them: DRAFT / NOT LIVE / DO NOT SEND.

9. Before and after illustrations

The letters below are fictional teaching examples. They are not real outreach, not research on any of the 17 creators, and not to be sent or adapted as if they described a real person. Channel names, video titles, and biographies are invented.

Example A — The swap-the-middle-sentence letter

Before (fails the swap test)

Hello, my name is Dean. I work on the marketing team at Rebind.

I've been following your content for a while and I'm a huge fan of your unique style. I loved your recent video about shortcuts — your audience is exactly who we want to reach.

Rebind is a next-level productivity platform that replaces clunky tools and supercharges your workflow with seamless, powerful automation. We'd love to explore a partnership.

Why it fails: only the words "video about shortcuts" are specific, and even those are not checkable. The product paragraph would be false on several fences (replaces, next-level, seamless). The offer has no geography, no editorial control, no disclosure, and no draft status. The CTA is vague. This letter could be dropped on anyone.

After (still a teaching sample, still not live)

Hello — my name is Dean. I work on the marketing team at Rebind.

I watched Stop Using Three Apps to Move One Window, where you write an AutoHotkey script that snaps a window, then you show the same idea breaking when you sit down at your Mac. I am not writing to replace AutoHotkey. People should keep using it. Rebind is a free app whose scripts are written in Luau and run on the computer. After you install the app, adding a script is a second step. A person can use it next to AutoHotkey, Keyboard Maestro, PowerToys, or a Stream Deck, including on Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) and on macOS 13 or later on Apple Silicon. It does not run on Linux, and Mac window control is only partial, which matters for the exact demo in that video.

If you are in the United States or Canada, we can offer to gift a Rebind Link so you can try the parts that need hardware, such as extra function keys. That sentence is draft copy only; it is not permission to ship anything. No video is required. If you did publish after receiving one, you would keep editorial control and should disclose the gift. We would talk through terms after you had used it.

If you want, reply with a yes, a no, or a question.

What changed: the video is named; the example depends on that video; the fences sit beside the promise; AutoHotkey is respected; the offer terms are in human prose next to the offer; the CTA is one easy answer. Swap the name and the video, and the middle of the letter stops making sense. That is the point.

Example B — Fake-fan voice versus earned detail

Before

Hello, my name is Dean. I work on the marketing team at Rebind. I've been a huge fan for years and your community is amazing. Your last upload blew me away. You really get what power users want.

After

Hello, my name is Dean. I work on the marketing team at Rebind. In VIA Isn't Magic, the Layer Is, you spend the middle third of the video showing a QMK layer that sends F13 on a key your OS does not expose, then you say the annoying part is doing anything useful with that key once it leaves the board. That is a real problem, and it is also a place I should be precise: in Rebind, F13 through F24 need Link or BYOH hardware. The free app alone will not invent those keys.

The after version does not claim a viewing history it does not have. It proves attention with a sequence from the video, then spends the earned goodwill on a limit rather than a superlative.

Example C — Empty superlatives and fake urgency

Before

Rebind is the ultimate everything-PC multitool. It is revolutionary, works on any computer, and just works, instantly, with no hardware required. Install it and you're fully set up. This is a limited-time chance to get a free Link before they are gone — act fast this week!

After

Rebind is a free app for device macros, automation, accessibility, and other computer-wide shortcuts. Scripts are Luau and they run on the computer. Local scripts do not require hardware or an account, but not every feature works without hardware, and many features are USB-only. Installing the app does not install a marketplace script; that is a second step. It supports Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit) and macOS 13 or later on Apple Silicon. Pointer movement, media keys, and F13 to F24 need Link or BYOH hardware. There is no deadline in this note, and nothing here authorizes a shipment.

The after version is longer because the truth is longer. That is allowed. Concise means no wasted words, not no necessary limits. [Verified-web] Strunk, Rule 13, Gutenberg text cited above.

Example D — A cloned legal block versus terms in the letter's own voice

Before

OPTIONAL GIFTED REBIND LINK OFFER — DRAFT TERMS: US/CA ONLY. NOT A SHIPMENT AUTHORIZATION. NO VIDEO REQUIRED. TERMS AFTER USE. CREATOR RETAINS EDITORIAL CONTROL. DISCLOSE GIFT IF YOU PUBLISH AFTER RECEIPT.

Those facts are correct and still read like a sticker applied to seventeen envelopes.

Same ingredients. Different sentences. The reader can hear a person.

10. Checklist: mark a letter draft-ready only after every box passes

Run this list yourself before you hand a letter up. If any box fails, the letter is not draft-ready.

Identity and form

  • The letter opens as Dean and states that he works on Rebind, on the marketing team.
  • The working file is marked DRAFT / NOT LIVE / DO NOT SEND.
  • There is exactly one low-friction reply CTA.
  • Files only: nothing in the package instructs anyone to send, post, or ship.

Channel knowledge

  • You opened at least one specific public video or equivalent public piece of work and recorded the URL and retrieved date in the working notes.
  • The letter names a checkable detail (a title, a format, a tool they teach, a moment they showed).
  • The swap test fails in the good way: removing that detail and changing the name would break the letter.
  • There is no fake-fan voice, no invented viewing history, and no generic praise that would fit anyone else on the list.

Product truth

  • The Rebind intro is honest and small, not a slogan pile.
  • The example is true and fenced. OS limits appear if compatibility is implied. Hardware limits appear if F13–F24, media keys, pointer movement, or other hardware-backed behavior appears.
  • Installing the app is not treated as installing a script or as activation.
  • The letter does not say Rebind replaces AutoHotkey, Keyboard Maestro, PowerToys, QMK, VIA, or Stream Deck.
  • No Linux, no Intel Mac, no medical device, no guaranteed accessibility outcome, no TypeTation destination, no .ahk drop-in, no automatic AI porting, no claim that Rebind can copy any existing AutoHotkey script or similar Lua-based peripheral script.
  • RebindGPT is not implied to be free. Mac window control is not implied to be complete.
  • Install URL, if used, is https://www.rebind.gg/install
  • Any price is either re-verified this turn or clearly labeled as prior team retrieval dated 2026-11-08.

Offer and disclosure (when the brief includes the Link offer)

  • The offer is described as optional and as draft copy only, not as shipment authorization.
  • US and Canada only.
  • No video required.
  • Terms after they have used it.
  • Creator keeps editorial control.
  • Disclose the gift if they publish after receiving one.
  • The net impression of the letter does not re-introduce an expected post, review, or mention.
  • You have not written claims a creator would be unable to disclose-and-repeat honestly (see 16 CFR 255.1(a) and 255.5). [Verified-web] eCFR page cited above.

Prose craft

  • Specific nouns outnumber abstract ones in the sentences that matter.
  • Empty superlatives and fake urgency are gone.
  • Ready-made strips of language (hope this finds you well, explore a partnership, supercharge your workflow) are gone.
  • Sentences vary in length and shape; the letter does not march in identical and/but pairs.
  • Required terms are present without a cloned all-caps block that makes the rest feel templated.
  • You read this draft next to at least one other letter in the batch; they do not share a skeleton.

Desk process

  • Brief restated; evidence notes saved; claim ledger completed.
  • Fact-check done against sources you opened.
  • marketing-claim-fence-qa run; no critical fence left open.
  • Packaged for review, not for send.

11. A revision pass you can do out loud

When a draft feels "fine" but not good, read it aloud and ask Orwell's questions, in ordinary language [Verified-web] Orwell Foundation page; applied here as [Inference]:

  1. What am I trying to say to this person, in this paragraph?
  2. Which words actually say it?
  3. Is there a concrete picture (a key, a video moment, a limit) or only a mood?
  4. Is that picture copied from marketing stock or taken from evidence?
  5. What can I cut?
  6. Did I write anything ugly, oily, or false?

Then do Strunk's shorter pass [Verified-web] Gutenberg text:

  • Change one abstract noun to a specific one.
  • Cut one needless phrase.
  • Put the most important word at the end of one sentence.
  • Break one monotonous sentence pattern.

If the paragraph still could be mailed to a different creator, delete the paragraph and start from the research note, not from the old sentence.

12. Skills to load by name

  • agency-os — The team's operating system. Files only unless Dean authorizes a named outbound action. Ads HOLD. No posts, DMs, emails, or spend. "Ship" means a complete, reviewable artifact, not going live. Label claims. Report to Agency CEO.
  • writing-team — Run writing as a desk: commission, source, draft, fact-check, copy-edit, claim-fence QA, package. A piece passes when a reader can see the job, the proof, the limits, and the next step, and when no claim depends on an invented source.
  • marketing-campaign-brief — Lock audience, job, promise, proof, exclusions, holds, and quality gates before drafting. If two strategies would both satisfy the brief, tighten it.
  • journalistic-marketing-writing — Use reported structure and sourced claims when the deliverable is longer-form marketing. Do not pretend to be a newspaper. State limits next to promises. Use-both framing for the named tools. No fake urgency and no invented social proof.
  • marketing-claim-fence-qa — Extract every objective claim, test it against Content Rules and the dossier, and fail the draft for the listed fence breaks. Do not mark a letter draft-ready while a critical fence is open.

Also use marketing-evidence-research when you are gathering creator or market facts: write the decision, write the questions, record URL and retrieved date, and label every finding.
